Pollution Lab

Live CAMS air-quality samples over the world's ~100 largest metros, an unsupervised machine-learning pass (weighted k-means) that identifies today's pollution centers, a scrubbable six-day CAMS history you can play back hour by hour, a wind-driven PM2.5 transport simulation on real GFS winds, and a disclosed aerosol radiative-forcing estimate of what that haze does to the planet's energy budget. Modeled data, inspectable math — every constant is on this page.

Winds are the current GFS snapshot held constant through the run — this explores dispersal patterns, it is not a forecast. Scrubbing does not move the winds. Methods & sources

Data. City air quality and the sparse background grid are CAMS global model fields sampled through Open-Meteo (modeled data, not station observations — the same provenance contract as the EarthView air-quality layer). Winds are the site's hourly 5° GFS grid. Wildfire events come from NASA EONET. All feeds are free and keyless; when one is down its pip goes red and, if nothing is live, the page runs on a clearly-badged bundled sample rather than pretending.

History window. The scrubber runs on /api/air-quality/history: one batched CAMS request returns an hourly PM2.5 series for the top 60 metros plus the sparse global background grid, resampled server-side onto a single shared time axis so frame i means the same instant for every site. Past hours are CAMS hindcast and future hours are CAMS forecast; the response marks the split and every chart on this page draws the forecast tail dashed. Missing hours arrive as nulls and stay nulls — statistics skip them and lines break at them, because a gap painted as clean air is invisible once rendered. Interpolating 145 frames would cost seconds of trig, so the inverse-distance weights are factored out once into a sparse operator (buildIdwOperator) and each frame is a matrix–vector product; it is bit-identical to the one-shot path, which the unit test asserts exactly.

Machine learning. Pollution centers are found by weighted k-means with farthest-point seeding and spherical centroids — deterministic on its input, so a reload gives identical clusters for identical data. The cluster count comes from a within-cluster variance elbow. The implementation is ~80 lines in js/pollution-model.js, unit-tested in tests/pollution-model.mjs.

Transport physics. The PM2.5 field evolves by semi-Lagrangian advection (unconditionally stable, monotone), explicit eddy diffusion, first-order deposition with adjustable lifetime τ, and a steady-source closure S = C/τ inferred from the observed field. 5° grid, 1 h steps.

Climate estimate. Excess PM2.5 over the WHO annual guideline is converted to AOD (85 µg/m³ per unit AOD), to top-of-atmosphere direct forcing (−25 W/m² per AOD), and to an equilibrium temperature response (λ = 0.8 K per W/m²). These are disclosed literature-scale constants; the estimate ignores indirect (cloud) effects, absorbing-aerosol warming, and regional feedbacks, and it says so.
