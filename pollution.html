Pollution Lab

Live CAMS air-quality samples over the world's ~100 largest metros, an unsupervised machine-learning pass (weighted k-means) that identifies today's pollution centers, a wind-driven PM2.5 transport simulation on real GFS winds, and a disclosed aerosol radiative-forcing estimate of what that haze does to the planet's energy budget. Modeled data, inspectable math — every constant is on this page.

Methods & sources

Data. City air quality and the sparse background grid are CAMS global model fields sampled through Open-Meteo (modeled data, not station observations — the same provenance contract as the EarthView air-quality layer). Winds are the site's hourly 5° GFS grid. Wildfire events come from NASA EONET. All feeds are free and keyless; when one is down its pip goes red and, if nothing is live, the page runs on a clearly-badged bundled sample rather than pretending.

Machine learning. Pollution centers are found by weighted k-means with farthest-point seeding and spherical centroids — deterministic on its input, so a reload gives identical clusters for identical data. The cluster count comes from a within-cluster variance elbow. The implementation is ~80 lines in js/pollution-model.js, unit-tested in tests/pollution-model.mjs.

Transport physics. The PM2.5 field evolves by semi-Lagrangian advection (unconditionally stable, monotone), explicit eddy diffusion, first-order deposition with adjustable lifetime τ, and a steady-source closure S = C/τ inferred from the observed field. 5° grid, 1 h steps.

Climate estimate. Excess PM2.5 over the WHO annual guideline is converted to AOD (85 µg/m³ per unit AOD), to top-of-atmosphere direct forcing (−25 W/m² per AOD), and to an equilibrium temperature response (λ = 0.8 K per W/m²). These are disclosed literature-scale constants; the estimate ignores indirect (cloud) effects, absorbing-aerosol warming, and regional feedbacks, and it says so.
