What is actually being solved

Every frame, this page solves height-integrated ionospheric current continuity for the electrostatic potential Φ on a 100×96 magnetic-latitude/MLT grid:

∇⊥ · ( Σ · ∇⊥Φ ) = −J∥ · sin(I)

Σ is the 2×2 conductance tensor built from Pedersen and Hall conductances (solar EUV via Moen–Brekke 1993, an auroral oval scaled to the driving, and a subauroral trough); J∥ is the field-aligned current — two Region-1 sheets driven instantaneously by the solar wind through the Kan–Lee merging electric field, and two Region-2 sheets that lag with time constant τs. That lag is the whole story: while R2 catches up, the high-latitude field leaks to low latitudes (undershielding); when the driver drops, the leftover R2 overshoots the other way (overshielding). This is the same equation the ionospheric electrodynamics component of the SWMF (the Ridley Ionosphere Model) solves inside NOAA's operational geospace model — here it runs in WebAssembly, in your browser, with a BiCGSTAB solver in double precision.

The equipotential contours are the E×B drift streamlines — plasma flows along them. The streaklets are advected by the solved drift field at physical speed on the same simulation clock as the solver; nothing is animated for effect.

SAPS — subauroral polarization streams — emerge rather than being drawn: Region-2 current closing across the low-conductance trough demands a large poleward electric field (same current, less conductance → more field), which drives a fast westward jet. Where |E| exceeds ~30 mV/m, frictional heating deepens the density trough, conductance drops further, and the jet self-sharpens — that feedback runs live (toggle it and watch the difference).

Drift-physics Region 2 (mini-RCM). The default R2 is a parameterized relaxation (τs slider). Switch to drift physics and R2 is instead computed the way the Rice Convection Model does it: six proton channels, each carrying a conserved energy invariant λ = W·V2/3, drift through the solved potential (E×B + corotation + gradient drift) from a plasma-sheet boundary scaled to the upstream wind. The field-aligned current is the Vasyliunas closure of the partial ring current's pressure asymmetry — nothing about R2's strength, location, or timing is imposed. Shielding still emerges (the test suite proves under- and overshielding without the ODE), and you can watch the violet pressure layer drift westward and symmetrize when the driving stops — which is why overshielding decays, not just that it decays.

Storm replays. The replay buttons drive every control from real OMNI 1-minute data through a committed hindcast bundle (the same `pp.hindcast.replay.v1` bundles the Hindcast Lab pages use). During a replay the validation panel compares this solver's CPCP against the full BATS-R-US + Ridley (SWMF IE) hindcast of the same storm — the two solve the same equation, one on a supercomputer, one in your browser tab — with the observed SYM-H alongside for storm-phase context.

LIVE mode. "Go live" drives the solver from NOAA SWPC's real-time solar wind, ballistically propagated from L1 to 32 RE — the upstream boundary of NOAA's operational Geospace model — so the propagated timestamps run ahead of the wall clock by roughly 30–60 minutes depending on wind speed; that gap is genuine forecast lead time, shown on the verdict card. The 1-minute feed is polled every 60 s and linearly interpolated to the solver's 10-second re-solve; Bz and By pass through a 3-sample median filter before the Kan–Lee coupling function so a single noisy sample cannot whipsaw the driving. Interpolation happens only between real samples — past the newest sample the drivers are held and flagged, after 10 minutes the card declares a DATA GAP, and after a 2-hour gap the Region-2 state is re-seeded toward equilibrium instead of integrating through garbage (the same cold-restart policy as the operational model). The verdict thresholds are not asserted: they are percentiles of this solver's own St. Patrick's 2015 replay (scripts/calibrate-shielding-verdict.mjs), re-bakeable when the Gannon bundle lands. Known simplifications — read before trusting

  • Electrostatic, height-integrated ionosphere; no neutral-wind dynamo. The model shows prompt penetration only — no disturbance dynamo, which dominates hours after storm onset.
  • Φ = 0 at 40° MLAT. Standard IE practice; the "penetration E" readout is taken at that boundary, not the true equator, so treat it as a shape/timing signal, not an absolute amplitude.
  • Region 2, default mode: parameterized (a relaxation toward α·IR1) — shielding morphology is imposed; the timing is emergent. The drift-physics mode removes that: R2 is the Vasyliunas current of a real drifting ring current. Its own honest limits: protons only (cold-electron closure), six energy channels, first-order upwind advection (numerically diffusive inner edge), a crude charge-exchange lifetime, and R1 stays parameterized — the solar-wind side of the circuit is not an MHD simulation.
  • Single hemisphere, aligned dipole, no seasons. Solar zenith angle uses magnetic coordinates directly; By enters the coupling function but does not yet rotate the convection pattern.
  • The density slider is a compression proxy — it nudges the oval and current sheets equatorward with dynamic pressure; it does not change the coupling function.
