# PART 1 — Holm 15A / Abell 85 primary literature & the origin of the '60 billion pair' claim


## Primary literature

- **"A 40 Billion Solar-mass Black Hole in the Extreme Core of Holm 15A, the Central Galaxy of Abell 85"** — Kianusch Mehrgan, Jens Thomas, Roberto Saglia, et al. (MPE/USM Munich); 2019; ApJ 887, 195; https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10608. The anchor paper: VLT/MUSE wide-field stellar kinematics plus orbit-based axisymmetric Schwarzschild modeling yields the most massive dynamically detected black hole in the local universe. Key numbers: M_BH = (4.0 ± 0.8) × 10^10 M_sun; z ≈ 0.055 (~216 Mpc, ~700 Mly); nearly flat velocity dispersion ~340 km/s; diffuse core "almost as large as the LMC," ~2 mag fainter centrally than any previously modeled depleted core; BH is 4–9x above M–sigma / M–M_bulge scaling predictions; orbits tangentially biased inside the core.

- **"The Brightest Cluster Galaxy in Abell 85: The Largest Core Known So Far"** — O. López-Cruz, C. Añorve, M. Birkinshaw, et al.; 2014; ApJL 795, L31; https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.7758. First flagged Holm 15A's extreme core and predicted an ultramassive BH from core-scaling relations. Key numbers: cusp radius r_gamma = 4.57 ± 0.06 kpc (4.26"), ~18x the BCG mean; sigma = 310 ± 15 km/s; inferred M_BH ~ 10^9–10^11 M_sun; z ≈ 0.055.

- **"Too Big to Be Real? No Depleted Core in Holm 15A"** — P. Bonfini, B. T. Dullo & A. W. Graham; 2015; ApJ 807, 136; https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.08560. First major challenge to the giant-core claim: a low-n Sérsic + outer exponential halo fits the light with no depleted core.

- **"The Abell 85 BCG: A Nucleated, Coreless Galaxy"** — Juan P. Madrid & Carlos J. Donzelli; 2016; ApJ 819, 50; https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.00078. Gemini GMOS r-band imaging found a nuclear light *excess* in the central kiloparsec — no light deficit — nullifying the depleted-core-based UMBH inference (pre-dynamical-measurement skepticism).

- **"The Growth of the Galaxy Cluster Abell 85: Mergers, Shocks, Stripping and Seeding of Clumping"** — Y. Ichinohe, N. Werner, A. Simionescu, et al.; 2015; MNRAS 448, 2971; https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1955. X-ray (XMM/Chandra/Suzaku) study of the host cluster's merger history. Key numbers: Abell 85 at z ≈ 0.055; ongoing subcluster mergers feeding the BCG.

- **"Structure of Brightest Cluster Galaxies and Intracluster Light"** — M. Kluge, R. Bender, A. Riffeser, et al.; 2020; ApJS 247, 43; arXiv:1908.08544. Deep Wendelstein WWFI g'-band photometry of 170 local BCGs including Holm 15A — the photometric basis underpinning Mehrgan et al.'s dynamical models. Surface-brightness limit ~30 mag/arcsec².

- **"AGN Jets and a Fanciful Trio of Black Holes in the Abell 85 Brightest Cluster Galaxy"** — Juan P. Madrid et al.; 2021; PASP 133, 014101; https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10774. Deep VLA radio imaging: the central radio structure is a pair of AGN jets from a SINGLE accreting BH; explicitly rebuts binary/trio interpretations of Holm 15A's center. Chandra X-ray data concur.

- **"A Spectroscopic Study of 14 Structures Behind Holm15A"** — Héctor Ibarra-Medel; 2022; MNRAS 515, 6032; https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11287. Background objects hidden in the BCG's diffuse light — caution for photometric/kinematic work on the ultra-low-surface-brightness core. Background compact group at z = 0.5814.

- **"A 22 Billion M_sun Black Hole in Holmberg 15A with Keck KCWI Spectroscopy and Triaxial Orbit Modeling"** — Emily R. Liepold, Chung-Pei Ma & Jonelle L. Walsh; 2025; ApJ 980, 58; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01493. Key follow-up: KCWI kinematics + triaxial TriOS Schwarzschild modeling (~40,000 models) roughly HALVES the Mehrgan mass, attributing the difference to triaxiality and joint fitting of M/L + dark halo + intrinsic shape. Key numbers: M_BH ≈ 2.2 × 10^10 M_sun vs 4.0 × 10^10 axisymmetric; still among the most massive dynamically measured BHs.

## Disentangling the "most massive pair, 60 billion, 4.4 billion ly" claim

- **"The Central Kinematics and Black Hole Mass of 4C+37.11 (B2 0402+379)"** — Tirth Surti, Roger W. Romani, et al.; 2024; ApJ; NOIRLab press: https://noirlab.edu/public/news/noirlab2405/. The genuinely measured "heaviest black hole PAIR" — but in B2 0402+379, not Abell 85, and less than half the popular claim's mass. Combined 28 × 10^9 M_sun; separation 24 ly (7.3 pc); ~750 Mly; merger stalled ~3 Gyr.

- **"A Kiloparsec-Scale Stellar Cavity in the Center of A402-BCG May Be Caused by Dynamic Interactions with an Ultramassive Black Hole"** — Michael McDonald et al. (MIT); 2026; ApJL 1002, L19; https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10104 (AAS Nova explainer: https://aasnova.org/2026/05/11/into-the-void-investigating-the-heart-of-a-giant-elliptical-galaxy/). **THE ACTUAL ORIGIN of the "60 billion combined, 4.4 billion light-years" claim** — JWST/NIRCam + HST + VLT/MUSE observations of the BCG of Abell 402 (NOT Abell 85) reveal a starless cavity and two candidate AGN consistent with an UMBH binary. Key numbers: cavity ~3,200 ly across; flattened 2.2 kpc core; ~2 × 10^9 M_sun of "missing" stars; single-BH interpretation (6 ± 4) × 10^10 M_sun; binary interpretation totaling 60 ± 20 × 10^9 M_sun; AGN relative velocity 370 km/s; ~4.4 billion ly.

- **"Astronomers May Have Found a Record-Breaking Pair of Black Holes"** — Science News; 2026; https://www.sciencenews.org/article/largest-pair-black-holes-collision. The popular article whose framing ("most massive pair… 60 billion… 4.4 billion light-years… Abell 402-BCG") is being garbled into an "Abell 85" claim in aggregators; the paper treats the binary as one of two possible interpretations.

- **"Holmberg 15A" — Wikipedia**; accessed 2026; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmberg_15A. Claims-tracker for the measurement history (López-Cruz 2014 core → Bonfini/Madrid skepticism → Mehrgan 2019 40B → Liepold 2025 22B).

## Key numbers consensus (agent synthesis)

Holm 15A is the BCG of Abell 85 at z ≈ 0.055 (~216 Mpc, ~700 million light-years — the LOCAL universe), σ ≈ 310–340 km/s, with an extraordinarily diffuse center: record 4.57 kpc depleted core (López-Cruz 2014) vs coreless low-n Sérsic (Bonfini 2015; Madrid & Donzelli 2016). Direct dynamical measurements bracket the BH at (4.0 ± 0.8) × 10^10 M_sun (Mehrgan 2019, axisymmetric) to ≈2.2 × 10^10 M_sun (Liepold 2025, triaxial), the factor-of-two driven by triaxiality and joint dark-halo/M-L fitting — either way a SINGLE UMBH. VLA + Chandra show one accreting BH with twin jets, no binary or trio (Madrid et al. 2021). The popular "most massive PAIR, combined 60 billion M_sun, 4.4 billion light-years" claim does NOT describe Holm 15A: it traces to McDonald et al. 2026 (ApJL 1002, L19) on **Abell 402-BCG**, where a 60 ± 20 billion M_sun *candidate* binary is one of two interpretations of a starless cavity, the other being a single (6 ± 4) × 10^10 M_sun BH. Attribution to Abell 85 is a conflation of two similarly named Abell BCGs with kpc-scale cores and ~10^10.5 M_sun black holes, reinforced by older binary-scouring speculation about Holm 15A and the confirmed-but-smaller pair in B2 0402+379 (28 billion combined, 750 Mly). TON 618 (66 billion, single quasar) probably cross-pollinates the "sixty-something billion" figure. Phoenix A's ~10^11 M_sun is an indirect, unconfirmed estimate matching neither the mass nor the distance.

# PART 2 — Observed and candidate SMBH binaries (the comparison set)


## A. B2 0402+379 / 4C +37.11 — the heaviest confirmed, closest-resolved binary

**1. The Central Kinematics and Black Hole Mass of 4C+37.11**
- Authors: Tirth Surti, Roger W. Romani, Julia Scharwächter, Alison Peck, Greg B. Taylor
- Year: 2024 | Venue: The Astrophysical Journal, 960, 110 (DOI: 10.3847/1538-4357/ad14fa)
- URL: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ad14fa | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07766
- Relevance: The definitive mass measurement of the heaviest known SMBH binary, using Gemini North GMOS IFU stellar kinematics and Jeans Anisotropic Modeling of the galaxy's scoured core; the primary calibration point for any ultramassive-pair simulation.
- Key numbers: Combined binary mass M = 2.8 (±0.8) × 10^10 M☉; VLBA-resolved projected separation 7.3 pc (~24 ly); host B2 0402+379 at z = 0.055 (~750 Mly), a "fossil cluster" elliptical; large depleted core r_b ≈ 1.5″; system apparently stalled for ≳3 Gyr.

**2. Constraining the Orbit of the Supermassive Black Hole Binary 0402+379**
- Authors: Karishma Bansal, Gregory B. Taylor, Robert T. Zavala, et al.
- Year: 2017 | Venue: The Astrophysical Journal, 843, 14
- URL: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/aa74e1 | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08556
- Relevance: First detection of relative orbital motion between two resolved SMBH cores — effectively the first SMBH "visual binary" — using 12 years of multi-frequency VLBI (5/8/15/22 GHz) with core-shift corrections.
- Key numbers: Projected separation 7.3 pc; relative velocity β = v/c = 0.0054 ± 0.0003 at PA −34.4°; inferred orbital period P ≈ 3 × 10^4 yr; dynamical combined mass M ≈ 1.5 × 10^10 M☉.

**3. A Compact Supermassive Binary Black Hole System (discovery paper)**
- Authors: Cristina Rodriguez, Gregory B. Taylor, Robert T. Zavala, A. B. Peck, L. K. Pollack, R. W. Romani
- Year: 2006 | Venue: The Astrophysical Journal, 646, 49
- URL: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/504825 | arXiv: astro-ph/0604042
- Relevance: VLBA discovery of the two flat-spectrum, variable compact cores (C1, C2) in 0402+379 — the original identification of the system as a resolved SMBH binary.
- Key numbers: Projected core separation 7.3 pc; z = 0.055; early combined-mass estimate ~1.5 × 10^8 M☉ from gas kinematics (since revised to ~10^10); estimated period ~1.5 × 10^5 yr.

**4. Astronomers Measure Heaviest Black Hole Pair Ever Found (press release)**
- Outlet: NSF NOIRLab / Gemini Observatory (release noirlab2405)
- Year: 2024 | URL: https://noirlab.edu/public/news/noirlab2405/
- Relevance: Accessible summary of the Surti/Romani result with the stalled-merger interpretation — the binary has ejected the stars that would drive it to coalescence; a direct observational anchor for final-parsec-problem physics.
- Key numbers: Combined mass 28 billion M☉; separation 24 light-years; stalled at that separation for over 3 billion years; host is a fossil-cluster elliptical.

## B. OJ 287 — the sub-parsec ultramassive prototype

**5. A massive binary black-hole system in OJ 287 and a test of general relativity**
- Authors: M. J. Valtonen, H. J. Lehto, K. Nilsson, et al.
- Year: 2008 | Venue: Nature, 452, 851–853 | URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06896
- Relevance: The canonical paper establishing OJ 287's precessing-binary model from the timing of its quasi-periodic optical outbursts (secondary impacting the primary's accretion disk).
- Key numbers: Primary mass ~1.8 × 10^10 M☉; secondary ~1.4–1.5 × 10^8 M☉; orbital period ~12 yr observed (rest-frame ~9 yr at z = 0.306); eccentricity e ≈ 0.66; orbital precession 39.1° ± 0.1 per orbit; semimajor axis ~0.05 pc.

**6. Refining the 2022 OJ 287 impact flare arrival epoch (first direct signal from the secondary)**
- Authors: Mauri J. Valtonen, Staszek Zola, et al.
- Year: 2023 | Venue: MNRAS, 521, 6143 | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15886
- Relevance: February 2022 day-long blue flare interpreted as the first direct detection of emission associated with the secondary black hole plunging through the primary's disk — validating the binary orbit solution's predictive power.
- Key numbers: Secondary 1.5 × 10^8 M☉ orbiting a 1.8 × 10^10 M☉ primary; flare duration ~1 day; z = 0.306.

**7. On the need of an ultramassive black hole in OJ 287**
- Authors: Mauri J. Valtonen, Staszek Zola, Achamveedu Gopakumar, et al.
- Year: 2023 | Venue: MNRAS, 525, 1153–1157 (DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stad2249) | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03017
- Relevance: Defends the ~18-billion-M☉ primary against proposed downward revisions (~10^8 M☉ alternatives) — important because the primary mass is contested in the literature.
- Key numbers: Primary 18.35 × 10^9 M☉ with a thin accretion disk; 130-yr optical light curve reproduced; ~12-yr outburst cycle.

## C. NGC 7727 — the closest confirmed SMBH pair

**8. First direct dynamical detection of a dual supermassive black hole system at sub-kiloparsec separation**
- Authors: Karina T. Voggel, Anil C. Seth, Holger Baumgardt, et al.
- Year: 2022 | Venue: Astronomy & Astrophysics, 658, A152 | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14854
- Relevance: The nearest and closest-separation dynamically confirmed dual SMBH — both masses measured directly from stellar kinematics (VLT/MUSE AO + HST mass models + Jeans modeling); defines the post-galaxy-merger, pre-hardening stage.
- Key numbers: Nucleus 1 BH: 1.54 (+0.18/−0.15) × 10^8 M☉; Nucleus 2 BH: 6.33 (+3.32/−1.40) × 10^6 M☉; projected separation ~500 pc (1,600 ly); distance ~27.4 Mpc (89 Mly); expected to merge within ~250 Myr.

**9. ESO telescope uncovers closest pair of supermassive black holes yet (press release eso2117)**
- Outlet: European Southern Observatory | Year: 2021 | URL: https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2117/
- Relevance: ESO's announcement of the Voggel et al. result; documents both distance records.
- Key numbers: Separation 1,600 ly; distance 89 Mly.

## D. Sub-parsec / imminent-merger candidates

**10. Tick-Tock: The Imminent Merger of a Supermassive Black Hole Binary (SDSS J1430+2303)**
- Authors: Ning Jiang, Huan Yang, Tinggui Wang, et al.
- Year: 2022 | Venue: arXiv preprint (not peer-review published) | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11633

## F. Population constraints — pulsar timing arrays

## Key numbers consensus (agent synthesis)

The most massive confirmed SMBH pair is B2 0402+379 / 4C +37.11, with a stellar-dynamical combined mass of 2.8 ± 0.8 × 10^10 M☉ (Surti/Romani et al. 2024), broadly consistent with the earlier VLBI-dynamical estimate of ~1.5 × 10^10 M☉ (Bansal et al. 2017), at a projected separation of 7.3 pc (~24 ly) with an inferred orbital period of ~3 × 10^4 yr — apparently stalled at that separation for ≳3 Gyr, a direct observational instance of the final-parsec problem. The second ultramassive anchor is OJ 287 (~1.8 × 10^10 M☉ primary + 1.5 × 10^8 M☉ secondary, 12-yr precessing e≈0.66 orbit at ~0.05 pc), though the primary mass is contested. The evolutionary sequence spans four decades in separation: kpc-scale dual AGN (3C 75 at ~7 kpc; Gaia/HST duals at 3.5–3.8 kpc at z≈2–3; NGC 7727 at ~500 pc), the single VLBI-resolved pc-scale binary (B2 0402+379, P ~ 10^4–10^5 yr), and sub-pc candidates identified only via periodicity (PKS 2131−021 at ~0.001–0.01 pc; OJ 287 at ~0.05 pc). Candidate reliability degrades sharply below a parsec — NGC 7674 was refuted by deeper imaging and SDSS J1430+2303's predicted merger never materialized — so the robust comparison tier is B2 0402+379, OJ 287, NGC 7727, PKS 2131−021. NANOGrav's background (A ≈ 2.4 × 10^−15 at 1 yr^−1) independently requires a substantial population of 10^8–10^10 M☉ binaries at sub-pc separations. An "ultramassive pair" simulation targeting ≳10^10 M☉ components sits at or just above the observed ceiling: no confirmed system exceeds B2 0402+379's ~2.8 × 10^10 M☉ combined mass, and the empirical expectation is that such a pair spends its longest observable phase stalled at parsec-scale separation before GW-driven inspiral takes over inside ~0.01–0.1 pc.

# PART 3 — Depleted stellar cores & core scouring


## Foundational theory

**1. Massive black hole binaries in active galactic nuclei**
- M. C. Begelman, R. D. Blandford, M. J. Rees — 1980 — Nature 287, 307–309 — https://www.nature.com/articles/287307a0
- The founding paper of SMBH binary evolution — galaxy mergers produce bound BH binaries that harden by slingshot-ejecting stars, then stall ("last parsec problem") before GW coalescence.
- Key numbers: Binary hardening stalls near ~1 pc separations; stars ejected at ~binary orbital velocity.

**2. Formation of Galactic Nuclei**
- M. Milosavljević, D. Merritt — 2001 — ApJ 563, 34–62 — https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/323830 (arXiv: astro-ph/0103350)
- First N-body simulations following an SMBH binary from galaxy merger to sub-parsec scales; the binary converts a steep central cusp into a shallow core by three-body slingshot ejection.
- Key numbers: Binary ejects a stellar mass of order its own combined mass per merger; core damage is cumulative — mass deficits grow roughly linearly with merger number.

**3. Massive Black Hole Binary Evolution**
- D. Merritt, M. Milosavljević — 2005 — Living Reviews in Relativity 8, 8 — arXiv: astro-ph/0410364
- Canonical review tying binary hardening, final-parsec problem, and coalescence to observable mass deficits — the depleted core as "fossil record" of binary scouring.
- Key numbers: Mass deficits of order 1× M_BH per merger; hardening radius a_h ≈ G·μ/(4σ²); cumulative deficits of a few M_BH from hierarchical merger trees.

## Core-Sérsic profile literature

**4. A New Empirical Model for the Structural Analysis of Early-Type Galaxies (core-Sérsic)**

- Introduces the core-Sérsic model (inner power law + outer Sérsic), the standard tool for measuring break radii and light deficits.
- Key numbers: core-Sérsic break radii typically tens of pc to ~1 kpc in luminous ellipticals.

**5. Evidence for a New Elliptical-Galaxy Paradigm: Sérsic and Core Galaxies**
- I. Trujillo, P. Erwin, A. Asensio Ramos, A. W. Graham — 2004 — AJ 127, 1917–1942 — https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0403659

**6. The Centers of Early-Type Galaxies with HST. IV. Central Parameter Relations**
- S. M. Faber et al. — 1997 — AJ 114, 1771 — https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9610055

## Mass deficit measurements

**7. Mass Deficits, Stalling Radii, and the Merger Histories of Elliptical Galaxies**
- D. Merritt — 2006 — ApJ 648, 976 — https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0603439

- Key numbers: M_def ≈ 0.5 M_12 (binary mass) per merger; after N mergers M_def ≈ 0.5·N·M_BH; observed deficits imply 1–3 major dry mergers for most core galaxies.

**8. Correlations Between SMBHs, Velocity Dispersions, and Mass Deficits in Elliptical Galaxies with Cores**

- Light/mass deficits correlate with σ and M_BH as tightly as the M–σ relation itself.

**9. Depleted Galaxy Cores and Dynamical Black Hole Masses**

- Key numbers: Mean M_def/M_BH ≈ 4.1 for core ellipticals; core ellipticals are the slowly rotating, boxy, dry-merger branch above ~2×10^11 M_sun.

## Dynamical fingerprints: tangential anisotropy and r_core–M_BH

**12. The Dynamical Fingerprint of Core Scouring in Massive Elliptical Galaxies**
- J. Thomas, R. P. Saglia, R. Bender, P. Erwin, M. Fabricius — 2014 — ApJ 782, 39 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3783

- Key numbers: Anisotropy β < 0 (tangential) inside r_b, switching to radial bias (β > 0) outside; transition radius coincides with the photometric break radius.

**13. A 17-billion-solar-mass black hole in a group galaxy with a diffuse core (NGC 1600)**

- Key numbers: M_BH = 1.7×10^10 M_sun (σ ≈ 293 km/s); r_SOI ≈ 1.2 kpc; core radius r_b ≈ 0.7 kpc; r_b ≈ r_SOI (≈1:1) across 21 core galaxies.

## N-body / simulation literature

**14. The Formation of Extremely Diffuse Galaxy Cores by Merging Supermassive Black Holes**
- A. Rantala, P. H. Johansson, T. Naab, J. Thomas, M. Frigo — 2018 — ApJ 864, 113 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10295
- KETJU equal-mass merger simulations tuned to NGC 1600: scouring simultaneously produces observed core size, low central surface brightness, and tangential anisotropy.
- Key numbers: Reproduces r_b ≈ 0.7 kpc with M_BH = 1.7×10^10 M_sun; progenitor density slope ρ ∝ r^(−3/2) favored; remnants follow observed r_b–r_SOI and r_b–M_BH relations.

**15. The Simultaneous Formation of Cored, Tangentially Biased, and Kinematically Decoupled Centers in Massive Early-type Galaxies**
- A. Rantala, P. H. Johansson, T. Naab, J. Thomas, M. Frigo — 2019 — ApJL 872, L17 — arXiv: 1812.02732
- Single mechanism — SMBH binary scouring in major mergers — explains cores, tangential bias, AND kinematically decoupled centers. This is the framework Mehrgan et al. ## Key numbers consensus (agent synthesis)

A single SMBH-binary scouring episode ejects roughly 0.5–1× the final BH mass in stars, so cumulative deficits scale as M_def ≈ 0.5·N·M_BH; observed deficits cluster at ~0.5–5 M_BH (implying 1–3, occasionally up to ~8, major dry mergers), with GW recoil able to add up to ~5 M_BH more. The core radius tracks the black hole almost one-to-one: r_b ≈ r_SOI = GM_BH/σ² (Thomas et al. 2016), and r_b correlates with M_BH more tightly than σ or luminosity does. Typical core galaxies have r_b ≈ 0.02–0.5 kpc; the large-core tail runs NGC 1600 (0.7 kpc) → A2261-BCG (r_γ = 3.2 kpc) → Holm 15A (r_γ = 4.57 kpc, core-Sérsic r_b ≈ 4 kpc). A single dry major merger cannot scour a core much larger than ~1 kpc (Nasim et al. 2021), so kpc-scale cores demand repeated mergers, GW-recoil oscillations, or — most efficiently — a merger of two galaxies whose cores were ALREADY depleted, which is exactly the Rantala et al. scenario that reproduces Holm 15A. Holm 15A's relative deficit (0.5–5.5 M_BH) is normal — the core is extreme only because the black hole is. Load-bearing simulation inputs: deficit-per-merger ≈ 0.5 M_BH; tangential anisotropy inside r_b, radial bias outside; r_b ≈ r_SOI; recoil-driven core growth up to ~5 M_BH for kicks below escape velocity.

# PART 4 — Binary inspiral dynamics, final-parsec problem, GW physics


## Stage 1 — Dynamical friction (kpc → ~10 pc)

**1. "Dynamical Friction. I. General Considerations"** — S. Chandrasekhar, 1943, ApJ 97, 255. DOI: 10.1086/144517
Foundational drag formula. F_df = −4πG²M²ρ lnΛ v⁻² [erf(X) − 2X e^(−X²)/√π], X = v/(√2σ); sinking time t_df ≈ (19 Gyr/lnΛ)(r/5 kpc)²(σ/200 km s⁻¹)(10⁸ M_⊙/M) — for a 2×10¹⁰ M_⊙ BH in a σ=346 km/s host: only ~20 Myr from 5 kpc (lnΛ≈8). Dynamical friction is FAST for ultramassive BHs.

**2. "Massive black hole binaries in active galactic nuclei"** — Begelman, Blandford & Rees, 1980, Nature 287, 307.
Canonical three-stage picture (dynamical friction → hard-binary/stellar hardening → GW inspiral); first identified possible stalling at ~parsec separations.

## Stage 2 — Hard binary, loss-cone depletion, final-parsec problem

**3. "Evolution of massive binary black holes"** — Q. Yu, 2002, MNRAS 331, 935. arXiv:astro-ph/0109530
Systematic loss-cone treatment across realistic galaxy models; binaries in spherical non-rotating galaxies generally stall after loss-cone depletion (t_relax >> t_Hubble in luminous galaxies); flattening/triaxiality can help.

**4. "The Final Parsec Problem"** — Milosavljević & Merritt, 2003, AIP Conf. Proc. 686, 201. arXiv:astro-ph/0212270
Named the problem. Key formulas: hard-binary separation a_h = Gm₂/4σ² (≈ 180 pc for an equal-mass 4×10¹⁰ M_⊙ binary at σ=346 km/s — "final parsec" is really "final 100 parsecs" at this mass); hardening rate d(1/a)/dt = H Gρ/σ with H ≈ 15–20; mass ejected ΔM_ej ~ J M_bin ln(a_h/a_GW).

**5. "Massive Black Hole Binary Evolution"** — Merritt & Milosavljević, 2005, Living Reviews in Relativity 8, 8. arXiv:astro-ph/0410364
Textbook-level review of the whole pipeline: dynamical friction, loss-cone theory (full vs empty regimes), slingshot kinematics, core scouring (M_def ≈ 0.5 M_bin per merger), Brownian motion of the binary, GW handoff. Best reference for validating a physics engine stage by stage.

## Stage 3 — Solutions to the final-parsec problem

**6. "Efficient Merger of Binary SMBHs in Nonaxisymmetric Galaxies"** — Berczik, Merritt, Spurzem & Bischof, 2006, ApJ 642, L21. arXiv:astro-ph/0601698
First N-body demonstration that rotating/triaxial models do NOT stall: centrophilic orbits keep the loss cone full, hardening N-independent, coalescence ≲10 Gyr without gas.

**7. "Efficient Merger of Binary SMBHs in Merging Galaxies"** — Khan, Just & Merritt, 2011, ApJ 732, 89 (arXiv:1103.0272); companion Khan, Holley-Bockelmann, Berczik & Just, 2013, ApJ 773, 100 (arXiv:1302.1871)
Merger remnants are naturally triaxial; hardening rates high and N-independent; coalescence ~1–4 Gyr after galaxies merge. 2013 extends to axisymmetric (flattened) nuclei. Target behavior: s ≡ d(1/a)/dt roughly constant in the hard phase.

**8. "The final-parsec problem in the collisionless limit"** — Vasiliev, Antonini & Merritt, 2015, ApJ 810, 49. arXiv:1505.05480
N→∞ convergence study: spherical → hardening → 0; axisymmetric → marginal; triaxial → a few-% departure from axisymmetry sustains hardening purely collisionlessly, coalescence in a few Gyr. Essential for choosing the correct effective hardening rate.

## Stage 4 — Gas-driven evolution / circumbinary disks

**9. "Accretion during the Merger of Supermassive Black Holes"** — Armitage & Natarajan, 2002, ApJ 567, L9. arXiv:astro-ph/0201318
Disk torques (type-II-like migration) shrink the binary on ~10⁷ yr timescales at ~0.1 pc; squeezed inner disk → enhanced accretion just before coalescence.

**10. "Accretion into the Central Cavity of a Circumbinary Disk"** — D'Orazio, Haiman & MacFadyen, 2013, MNRAS 436, 2997. arXiv:1210.0536
2D hydro over 0.003 ≤ q ≤ 1: cavity r_cav ≈ 2a but accretion streams persist; time-averaged accretion suppressed ≤~5×; periodicities at ~1×, 2× t_orb and the "lump" period.

**11. "Binary Black Hole Accretion from a Circumbinary Disk"** — Farris, Duffell, MacFadyen & Haiman, 2014, ApJ 783, 134. arXiv:1310.0492
Moving-mesh sims resolving persistent minidisks fed by cavity streams (0.026 ≤ q ≤ 1); accretion preferentially feeds the secondary (drives q → 1). (For a dry-merger elliptical, gas torques likely subdominant to stellar hardening.)

## Stage 5 — GW-driven inspiral

**12. "Gravitational Radiation and the Motion of Two Point Masses"** — P. C. Peters, 1964, Phys. Rev. 136, B1224.
THE essential equations: ⟨da/dt⟩ = −(64/5)G³m₁m₂M/(c⁵a³)·(1−e²)^(−7/2)(1 + 73e²/24 + 37e⁴/96); ⟨de/dt⟩ = −(304/15)e G³m₁m₂M/(c⁵a⁴)(1−e²)^(−5/2)(1 + 121e²/304). Circular coalescence time t_c = (5/256)c⁵a⁴/(G³m₁m₂M) ∝ a⁴/M³; eccentricity accelerates coalescence by ≈(1−e²)^(7/2) (e=0.9 → ~10³× faster).

## Stage 6 — Numerical relativity: remnant mass, spin, recoil kicks

**13. "Maximum Gravitational Recoil"** — Campanelli, Lousto, Zlochower & Merritt, 2007, PRL 98, 231102. arXiv:gr-qc/0702133
Superkick discovery: v_kick ∝ q²/(1+q)⁵ · |S⃗₂/m₂ − qS⃗₁/m₁| · cos(Θ), up to ~4000 km/s for maximal anti-aligned in-plane spins (vs ≤175 km/s non-spinning).

**14. "Hangup Kicks"** — Lousto & Zlochower, 2011, PRL 107, 231102. arXiv:1108.2009
Partially aligned spins → maximum kicks ~5000 km/s. Remnant-property program: equal-mass non-spinning remnant a_f ≈ 0.69, E_rad ≈ 4.8% Mc² — for a 4×10¹⁰ M_⊙ merger, ~2×10⁹ M_⊙ radiated.

## Stage 7 — Numerical methods: regularized N-body codes

**15. "Implementing Few-Body Algorithmic Regularization with PN Terms"** — Mikkola & Merritt, 2008, AJ 135, 2398. arXiv:0709.3367
AR-CHAIN: chain coordinates + log-Hamiltonian time-transformed leapfrog + GBS extrapolation; velocity-dependent (PN through 2.5PN) forces; no singularities.

**16. "High performance massively parallel direct N-body simulations on large GPU clusters" (φ-GPU)** — Berczik, Nitadori, Zhong, Spurzem, Hamada, et al., 2011. https://github.com/berczik/phi-GPU-mole
MPI/CUDA direct-summation Hermite code (4th/6th/8th order, block timesteps); up to ~6×10⁶ bodies; used for the Khan et al. hardening runs.

**17. "Post-Newtonian Dynamical Modeling of SMBHs in Galactic-scale Simulations" (KETJU)** — Rantala, Pihajoki, Johansson, Naab, Lahén & Sawala, 2017, ApJ 840, 53. arXiv:1611.07028
AR-CHAIN regularized regions around each SMBH inside GADGET-3 tree code; PN through 3.5PN; GW-timescale merger criterion; follows binaries to ~0.005 pc in a live galaxy.

**18. "MSTAR — minimum spanning tree regularized integrator"** — Rantala, Pihajoki, Mannerkoski, Johansson & Naab, 2020, MNRAS 492, 4131. arXiv:2001.03180
Successor to AR-CHAIN: MST coordinates + two-fold parallelization; |ΔE/E| ~ 10⁻¹⁴; thousands of particles inside the regularized region.

## Stage 8 — Detectability: PTA band

**19. "The NANOGrav 15 yr Data Set: Evidence for a Gravitational-wave Background"** — Agazie et al., 2023, ApJL 951, L8. arXiv:2306.16213
Hellings–Downs-correlated background, A ≈ 2.4×10⁻¹⁵ at 1 yr⁻¹, h_c ∝ f^(−2/3). Band: ~1–100 nHz. A 4×10¹⁰ M_⊙ binary has f_ISCO ≈ 110 nHz — it inspirals and merges INSIDE the PTA band and never reaches LISA (10⁻⁴–10⁻¹ Hz, for 10⁴–10⁷ M_⊙ mergers).

## Target-system anchor

**20. "A 40-billion solar mass black hole in the extreme core of Holm 15A"** — Mehrgan et al., 2019, ApJ 887, 195. arXiv:1907.10608
M_BH = (4.0±0.8)×10¹⁰ M_⊙, σ ≈ 346 km/s, largest known depleted stellar core. Boundary conditions for the simulation's fiducial system.

## Key physics for the simulation (agent synthesis)

A simulation of a Holm 15A-like binary (σ ≈ 346 km/s, M_tot ≈ 4×10¹⁰ M_⊙, equal-mass) must chain five regimes:
(1) Chandrasekhar dynamical friction brings each BH from ~5 kpc to center in t_df ~ 20 Myr at this mass — fast; influence radius r_infl = GM/σ² ≈ 1.4 kpc.
(2) Binary becomes "hard" at a_h = Gm₂/4σ² ≈ 180 pc; engine must switch from bulk friction to three-body slingshot hardening at d(1/a)/dt = H Gρ/σ, H ≈ 15–20, ejecting ~M_bin in stars and scouring the core (M_def ≈ 0.5 M_bin per merger).
(3) Peters (1964) GW decay takes over where t_GW < t_harden; a circular binary coalesces within 10 Gyr already from a_GW ≈ 4 pc, so the stellar stage only needs to close a factor ~45 gap (180 → 4 pc); e = 0.9 shrinks t_GW by ~10³.
(4) Whether the gap closes hinges on loss-cone physics: need triaxial/rotating potentials with centrophilic orbits (spherical collisionless refilling stalls); gas/circumbinary-disk torque module (~10⁷ yr migration at 0.1 pc) optional and likely subdominant for a dry elliptical.
(5) Relativistic endgame: PN through 2.5–3.5PN in a regularized integrator, then NR-calibrated merger: remnant spin a_f ≈ 0.69, ~5% mass loss (~2×10⁹ M_⊙ radiated) for equal-mass non-spinning, recoil kick from Campanelli/Lousto–Zlochower formulas (≤175 km/s non-spinning, up to ~4000–5000 km/s superkick/hangup configurations, vs central escape speed ~2000 km/s — extreme kicks can EJECT the remnant; typical ones make it slosh, further flattening the core).
Observationally the binary sweeps the PTA band (1–100 nHz; a ≈ 0.06 pc at f_GW = 10 nHz) and merges at f_ISCO ≈ 110 nHz with horizon scale r_s ≈ 2GM/c² ≈ 0.004 pc ≈ 800 AU — a resolvable continuous-wave PTA source, entirely below the LISA band.

# PART 6 — Numerical & rendering methods for a browser-based simulation


## 1. N-body integrators (star clusters around SMBH binaries)

**1. [Post-Newtonian Dynamical Modeling of Supermassive Black Holes in Galactic-scale Simulations (KETJU)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07028)** — A. Rantala, P. Pihajoki, P. H. Johansson, T. Naab, N. Lahén, T. Sawala. 2017. ApJ 840, 53. arXiv:1611.07028.
Defines the reference architecture for coupling a cheap global integrator to an expensive accurate one: algorithmically regularized (AR-CHAIN) regions around each SMBH embedded inside a tree/leapfrog galaxy simulation, with PN corrections between SMBHs and a merger criterion at ~0.005 pc based on GW coalescence timescale. *Algorithmic facts:* hybrid scheme — O(N log N) tree outside, O(N_chain²) regularized chain inside a switching radius; PN terms (incl. 2.5PN radiation reaction) applied only to SMBH pairs; demonstrates exactly the "switch integrators by regime" pattern a web sim should copy at smaller scale.

**2. [Implementing Few-Body Algorithmic Regularization with Post-Newtonian Terms (AR-CHAIN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.3367)** — S. Mikkola, D. Merritt. 2008. AJ 135, 2398–2405. arXiv:0709.3367. ([IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0004-6256/135/6/2398))
The canonical few-body regularized integrator for relativistic SMBH binaries: chain coordinates + logarithmic-Hamiltonian time-transformed leapfrog + Gragg–Bulirsch–Stoer extrapolation, handling velocity-dependent perturbations including PN terms up to 2.5PN. *Algorithmic facts:* machine-precision accuracy through close encounters with no KS transformation; explicit leapfrog kept valid for velocity-dependent forces via an auxiliary-velocity ("generalized midpoint") trick; cost O(N²) per substep, practical for N ≲ few hundred — fine for the innermost bound cluster in a browser.

**3. [Algorithmic regularization with velocity-dependent forces](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0605054)** — S. Mikkola, D. Merritt. 2006. MNRAS 372, 219. arXiv:astro-ph/0605054.
The paper that made time-transformed leapfrog usable with PN (velocity-dependent) accelerations — the auxiliary-velocity algorithm here is small enough to transcribe directly into JavaScript. *Algorithmic facts:* second-order leapfrog kernel boosted to high order by extrapolation; avoids implicit iteration for velocity-dependent forces; the enabling trick behind AR-CHAIN and KETJU.

**4. [On a Hermite Integrator with Ahmad–Cohen Scheme for Gravitational Many-Body Problems](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992PASJ...44..141M/abstract)** — J. Makino, S. J. Aarseth. 1992. PASJ 44, 141–151.
The standard 4th-order Hermite predictor–corrector with individual/block timesteps used by essentially all collisional N-body codes; the pragmatic choice if the web sim needs better-than-leapfrog accuracy for stars on eccentric orbits near the binary. *Algorithmic facts:* 4th-order using only force and its first time derivative (both analytic for 1/r²); block timesteps give per-particle adaptivity; direct-sum O(N²) forces — tractable in WASM up to N ~ 10⁴ at interactive rates.

**5. [N-body simulations of gravitational dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1082)** — W. Dehnen, J. I. Read. 2011. Eur. Phys. J. Plus 126, 55. arXiv:1105.1082.
Best single review for implementing web-scale N-body correctly: symplectic leapfrog (KDK) and why it conserves energy, softening choices, timestep criteria, tree methods. *Algorithmic facts:* leapfrog is 2nd-order symplectic and time-reversible; adaptive timesteps break symplecticity unless chosen time-symmetrically; tree codes reduce cost to O(N log N) — relevant if the star field grows beyond ~10⁴.

**6. [Building a Better Leapfrog](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...443L..93H/abstract)** — P. Hut, J. Makino, S. McMillan. 1995. ApJ 443, L93–L96.
Four-page letter on making leapfrog time-symmetric with individual adaptive timesteps — the cheapest respectable integrator upgrade for a JS main loop. *Algorithmic facts:* 2nd order; time-symmetrized step selection eliminates secular energy drift with variable steps; near-zero implementation overhead over plain leapfrog.

## 2. Post-Newtonian dynamics

**7. [Gravitational Radiation from Post-Newtonian Sources and Inspiralling Compact Binaries](https://link.springer.com/article/10.12942/lrr-2014-2)** — L. Blanchet. 2014. Living Reviews in Relativity 17, 2. arXiv:1310.1528. (Updated edition: [Post-Newtonian theory for gravitational waves, LRR 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41114-024-00050-z).)
The authoritative, open-access source of explicit PN acceleration terms — the 1PN/2PN conservative and 2.5PN radiation-reaction accelerations in harmonic coordinates can be copied verbatim into a WASM force routine. *Algorithmic facts:* equations of motion and flux through 3.5PN+ order; radiation reaction first enters at 2.5PN (order (v/c)⁵); PN expansion valid only for separations ≳ 10 GM/c² — the sim must hand off to a phenomenological merger model below that.

## 3. Peters orbit-averaged GW inspiral

**8. [Gravitational Radiation and the Motion of Two Point Masses](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.136.B1224)** — P. C. Peters. 1964. Phys. Rev. 136, B1224–B1232.
The workhorse: orbit-averaged ⟨da/dt⟩ and ⟨de/dt⟩ ODEs and the closed-form circular coalescence time T = a⁴/4β — this is the coupling that shrinks the binary separation over compressed time in the sim. *Algorithmic facts:* two coupled first-order ODEs, trivially integrated with RK4 per frame; assumes Keplerian orbits + quadrupole radiation, so valid only while the orbit is adiabatic (separation ≳ tens of GM/c²); predicts circularization (e→0), justifying a circular-orbit endgame.

**9. [Gravitational Radiation from Point Masses in a Keplerian Orbit](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.131.435)** — P. C. Peters, J. Mathews. 1963. Phys. Rev. 131, 435–440.
Companion paper giving the radiated power per orbital harmonic and the eccentricity enhancement factor f(e) — exactly what's needed to synthesize an honest strain/audio readout for an eccentric binary. *Algorithmic facts:* power in the nth harmonic in closed form (Bessel functions); f(e) multiplies the circular-orbit luminosity; cheap to evaluate per frame.

## 4. Relativistic ray-tracing / real-time rendering

**10. [Gravitational Lensing by Spinning Black Holes in Astrophysics, and in the Movie Interstellar (DNGR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03808)** — O. James, E. von Tunzelmann, P. Franklin, K. S. Thorne. 2015. Class. Quantum Grav. 32, 065001. arXiv:1502.03808.
The visual ground truth for a lensed accretion disk around a Kerr BH (the "Gargantua" above/below-disk double image) and the reference for ray-bundle (beam) propagation that avoids star-field flickering. *Algorithmic facts:* propagates elliptical ray bundles, not single rays, through Kerr; offline renderer (~CPU-hours per IMAX frame) — use as correctness reference, not as the runtime method; documents the deliberate omission of Doppler beaming for aesthetics (a toggle the web sim should expose).

**11. [Seeing relativity — I. Ray tracing in a Schwarzschild metric...and making a proper rendering of the stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06025)** — A. Riazuelo. 2019. Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 28, 1950042. arXiv:1511.06025.
Meticulous treatment of how to render the *star field* correctly near a black hole — aberration, Doppler shift, amplification, gravitational redshift for an arbitrarily moving observer — the checklist for the sim's background-sky shader. *Algorithmic facts:* Schwarzschild-only; full geodesic integration; establishes which special-relativistic observer effects matter visually and how to color-shift stars physically.

**12. [Interactive Black-Hole Visualization](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/interactive-black-hole-visualization/)** — A. Verbraeck, E. Eisemann. 2021. IEEE TVCG 27(2), 796–805. (IEEE VIS SciVis Best Paper.)
The closest published method to what a WebGL implementation should do for Kerr: precompute geodesic deflections into an adaptive grid mapping the observer's 360° view to the distorted celestial sphere, then interpolate on the GPU in real time — including a moving observer. *Algorithmic facts:* two-step precompute+interpolate design; adaptive grid refined where distortion gradients are large; real-time on GPU with real star catalogs; interpolation error bounded and analyzed.

**13. [Real-time High-Quality Rendering of Non-Rotating Black Holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08735)** — E. Bruneton. 2020. arXiv:2010.08735. ([Open-source GLSL shader + demo](https://ebruneton.github.io/black_hole_shader/))
The single most directly reusable asset: a documented, open-source GLSL fragment shader that renders a Schwarzschild BH with accretion disk, lensed stars, Doppler/beaming, in constant time per pixel via precomputed geodesic lookup textures — portable to WebGL2 nearly as-is. *Algorithmic facts:* 2D precomputed texture parameterized by ray impact parameters replaces per-pixel geodesic integration; beam tracing with texture filtering integrates light-source contributions; non-rotating only (acceptable visual approximation for most SMBH spins at web fidelity).

## 5. Accretion-disk and shadow appearance (ground truth)

**14. [Image of a spherical black hole with thin accretion disk](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1979A%26A....75..228L/abstract)** — J.-P. Luminet. 1979. Astron. Astrophys. 75, 228–235.
The original computed photograph: primary and secondary (lensed) disk images and the strong Doppler brightness asymmetry — the checklist of features a "physically honest" web rendering must reproduce. *Algorithmic facts:* semi-analytic Schwarzschild geodesics + Page–Thorne thin-disk emissivity; disk inner edge at ISCO (6 GM/c²); approaching side several times brighter than receding side.

**15. [First M87 Event Horizon Telescope Results. I. The Shadow of the Supermassive Black Hole](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11238)** — EHT Collaboration. 2019. ApJL 875, L1. arXiv:1906.11238.
Observational ground truth for what a ~10⁹–10¹⁰ M☉ SMBH actually looks like: a photon ring of diameter ~√27 GM/c² (42 μas for M87*'s 6.5×10⁹ M☉) with rotation-induced brightness asymmetry — calibrates the shadow-to-mass scaling for the sim's ~10¹⁰ M☉ holes. *Algorithmic facts:* shadow diameter ≈ 9.6–10.4 GM/c² nearly independent of spin (≤4% variation) — so a Schwarzschild shader is quantitatively defensible; asymmetric ring from Doppler beaming of orbiting plasma.

## 6. Circumbinary disk morphology

**16. [Binary Black Hole Accretion from a Circumbinary Disk: Gas Dynamics Inside the Central Cavity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.0492)** — B. D. Farris, P. Duffell, A. I. MacFadyen, Z. Haiman. 2014. ApJ 783, 134. arXiv:1310.0492.
The paper whose surface-density figures are the de facto visual template for binary accretion: low-density central cavity, two accretion streams peeling off the inner edge, persistent "minidisks" around each hole, and an orbiting overdense lump. *Algorithmic facts:* 2D viscous Navier–Stokes on the DISCO moving (shearing) mesh; cavity radius ~2× binary semimajor axis; accretion modulated at harmonics of the binary period (lump period ~5× binary) — these ratios can be baked into a procedural disk texture/particle system without running hydro in the browser.

**17. [An Eccentric Circumbinary Accretion Disk and the Detection of Binary Massive Black Holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0607467)** — A. I. MacFadyen, M. Milosavljević. 2008. ApJ 672, 83–93. arXiv:astro-ph/0607467.
Establishes the persistent features a cheap visual model should encode: cavity of radius ≈ 2a with density suppressed by orders of magnitude, an inner disk edge that becomes eccentric, and a two-armed spiral density wave stationary in the binary's corotating frame. *Algorithmic facts:* 2D grid-based, locally isothermal viscous hydro, equal-mass circular binary; accretion across the cavity is quasi-periodic — supports driving disk brightness flicker from binary phase.

## 7. GW waveform approximants (strain/audio readout)

**18. [Frequency-domain gravitational waves from non-precessing black-hole binaries. II. A phenomenological model for the advanced detector era (IMRPhenomD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07253)** — S. Khan, S. Husa, M. Hannam, F. Ohme, M. Pürrer, X. Jiménez Forteza, A. Bohé. 2016. Phys. Rev. D 93, 044007. arXiv:1508.07253. (Companion Paper I: Husa et al., arXiv:1508.07250.)
The full inspiral–merger–ringdown waveform as closed-form piecewise analytic amplitude and phase in the frequency domain — evaluable in microseconds in plain JavaScript, ideal for a live strain plot and (frequency-shifted) audio chirp. *Algorithmic facts:* aligned-spin only; calibrated to mass ratios up to 1:18 and |χ| ≲ 0.85; mismatch <1% against NR hybrids; waveform scales trivially with total mass (f ∝ 1/M) — note f_ISCO ~ 10⁻⁷ Hz at 4×10¹⁰ M☉, so audio requires an explicit frequency-shift factor (~10⁹×) that the UI should disclose.

## 8. Timescales, scale range, and time compression

**19. [Massive black hole binaries in active galactic nuclei](https://www.nature.com/articles/287307a0)** — M. C. Begelman, R. D. Blandford, M. J. Rees. 1980. Nature 287, 307–309.
The canonical three-stage evolution (dynamical friction → three-body hardening/"final parsec" → GW inspiral) and the source of the ~10⁸–10⁹ yr residence timescales — the physical justification for the sim's time-compression design and its stage-switching state machine. *Algorithmic facts:* each stage has an analytic hardening/decay rate, so the sim can advance "era time" with per-stage closed-form formulas rather than integrating through 10⁸ yr of orbits.

**20. [Gaia Sky: Navigating the Gaia Catalog](https://tonisagrista.com/papers/)** — A. Sagristà, S. Jordan, T. Müller, F. Sadlo. 2019. IEEE TVCG 25(1). DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2018.2864508.
Engineering reference for real-time astronomy visualization across extreme dynamic ranges — floating camera origin, logarithmic depth buffering, magnitude-space navigation, relativistic camera and gravitational-wave display effects, in an OpenGL app whose techniques transfer directly to WebGL. *Algorithmic facts:* logarithmic depth buffer + camera-relative coordinates solve float32 precision over ~10 orders of magnitude of scale — the same problem a parsec-scale-binary-to-horizon-scale-shadow sim faces.

## Implementation notes (agent synthesis)

For a browser simulation of a ~4×10¹⁰ M☉ binary (GM/c² ≈ 0.002 pc ≈ 400 AU per hole), the minimal defensible stack is a three-layer physics model with explicit regime hand-offs, in the spirit of KETJU but drastically simplified. Integrate the two SMBHs as an isolated 2-body problem in WASM using leapfrog/KDK (time-symmetrized per Hut–Makino–McMillan) with 1PN + 2PN conservative and 2.5PN radiation-reaction accelerations transcribed from Blanchet's Living Review, using the Mikkola–Merritt auxiliary-velocity trick so leapfrog stays explicit with velocity-dependent forces; treat the surrounding star cluster (N ~ 2–5×10³) as test particles in the binary's time-dependent Newtonian potential, which drops the cost from O(N²) to O(N) and is physically adequate since the binary mass dominates. Do not integrate the actual ~10⁸ yr inspiral: run the Peters (1964) ⟨da/dt⟩, ⟨de/dt⟩ ODEs as the master clock, mapping wall-clock time to remaining coalescence time logarithmically (per BBR 1980's staged timescales) so early hardening plays at ~10⁵ yr/s while the last orbits and plunge resolve to real PN dynamics — switch from Peters-driven separation to the live PN integrator when a/(GM/c²) drops below ~50, and cut to an IMRPhenomD-style merger-ringdown (closed-form, trivially evaluated in JS) below ~10. For rendering, port Bruneton's open-source precomputed-texture Schwarzschild shader to WebGL2 — one lookup-texture set shared by both holes, composited with a screen-space approximation for mutual lensing when the holes' Einstein radii overlap — since EHT Paper I shows the shadow shape is spin-insensitive to ≤4%, making the non-rotating approximation quantitatively defensible; Verbraeck–Eisemann's adaptive-grid method is the upgrade path if Kerr fidelity is later wanted. Skin the gas as a procedural circumbinary disk encoding Farris/MacFadyen morphology (cavity radius 2a, two trailing streams, per-hole minidisks, an orbiting lump modulating brightness at ~5 binary periods) with Luminet/DNGR-style lensed primary+secondary disk images from the shader. Drive the strain plot and audio from Peters–Mathews harmonics during inspiral and the PhenomD amplitude/phase through merger, frequency-shifted by an explicit, displayed factor (~10⁹) since the physical signal peaks near 10⁻⁷ Hz. Use camera-relative float origins and a logarithmic depth buffer (Gaia Sky) to span the parsec-to-horizon scale range without precision artifacts.
