The problem, and the receipt

The atmosphere model is wrong in the direction that loses spacecraft

Case 01 — Starlink Group 4-7 · Feb 2022

38 satellites lost to drag the model didn't see

A modest geomagnetic storm raised thermospheric density shortly after launch. Atmospheric drag came in well above what the operational empirical model predicted. Of 49 newly launched Starlink satellites, 38 failed to climb to operational orbit and reentered.

The model wasn't off by a rounding error. It was off in the one direction that de-orbits hardware — and empirical models degrade exactly when storms arrive.

The differentiator

Why physics-first beats the black box

The space situational awareness market is crowded with machine-learning models. Here's what a first-principles approach gives you that curve-fitting can't.

01

MHD, not curve-fit

Global magnetosphere modeling in the SWMF / BATS-R-US lineage — not empirical models like NRLMSIS, calibrated to quiet conditions and degrading exactly when a storm arrives.

02

Transparent & reproducible

Every model traces to a published equation. A program officer can evaluate the method itself; a result can be defended in technical review. No black box to take on faith.

03

Validated on real storms

Tested against the events that break empirical models — Starlink 2022, Gannon 2024 — not just quiet-time baselines that any model can fit.
