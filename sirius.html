Physics Notes▼

Sirius A is an A1V main-sequence star, 25× more luminous than the Sun. Its blue-white light makes it appear the brightest star in the sky (apparent magnitude −1.46) despite being only 2.64 pc away.

Sirius B is a DA2 white dwarf — the collapsed remnant of a star that was once ~5 M☉. At 25,200 K it glows hot blue-white, but with only 0.0084 R☉ radius it appears tiny. It was discovered in 1862 by Alvan Clark.

Gravity: Surface gravity of Sirius B is ~330,000× Earth's. A teaspoon of white dwarf material would weigh ~15 tonnes.

Eccentric orbit: The two stars orbit a common barycentre with eccentricity 0.591 — separation varies from 8.08 AU (periapsis) to 31.49 AU (apoapsis).

Physics Engine (WASM)▼

Orbit: Kepler equation solved with Danby starter + cubic Halley-Newton refinement (<1e-14 residual). 1PN Schwarzschild periastron precession Δω ≈ 9.2 mas/orbit (Damour–Deruelle 1985).

Gravitational waves: Peters–Mathews (1963) quadrupole. f(e=0.591) ≈ 9.52, ⟨dE/dt⟩ ≈ 9.3×10¹⁵ erg/s. Strain h ≈ 10⁻²² at Earth, frequency ~1.3 nHz (sub-pulsar-timing-array). Decay time tGW ≈ 10²¹ yr.

Wind & accretion: Sirius A drives a weak A-star wind (Ṁ ≈ 10⁻¹³ M☉/yr, v∞ ≈ 600 km/s — Landstreet+ 2011). Bondi–Hoyle–Lyttleton capture onto Sirius B with ρwind∝r⁻² gives ≈ 15× modulation between peri and apo passes.

Plasma shock: vff = √(2GMB/RB) ≈ 6800 km/s; strong-shock Ts ≈ 6×10⁸ K (~50 keV hard X-ray). Bremsstrahlung emissivity εff,ν = 6.8×10⁻³⁸ neniT⁻¹ᐟ²e⁻ʰᵛ/ᵏᵀ (Rybicki & Lightman §5).

Sirius B atmosphere (DA): Eddington-Barbier emergent flux Fν = πBν(T(τν=2/3)) on a grey T(τ) stratification, with H bound-free opacity at the Lyman edge (912 Å) and Balmer edge (3650 Å). EUV brightening ≈ 50× over naïve Planck — recovers the observed soft X-ray excess (Beuermann+ 2006). Lyα–Lyζ absorption lines via Stark-broadened Lorentzians.
