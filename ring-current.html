The 3D scene is a true GSM frame: Earth’s spin phase and axial tilt follow the live subsolar point, the magnetosphere tilts by the live dipole tilt angle ψ (IGRF dipole axis + low-precision solar ephemeris), and particle bounce runs at its physical period — only drift is time-compressed, and 1× makes the whole scene real time. Particle lifetimes are physical: charge exchange against the Rairden (1986) geocoronal H profile with Lindsay–Stebbings-class cross-sections (O⁺ ∼10× shorter-lived than H⁺ at 100 keV — the two-phase recovery); deep-mirror ions and electrons precipitate. Time & scale: one simulation clock τ drives every population at its true physical velocity. The near-Earth region is spatially linear (1 unit = 1 Rᴇ = 6,371 km); the L1→magnetopause corridor is compressed ≈5.5× (1 unit ≈ 34,900 km), so the on-screen stream:ring speed ratio is ≈6:1 rather than the physical ≈100:1 — disclosed here instead of hidden in animation speeds. Data: NOAA SWPC real-time solar wind (DSCOVR/ACE at L1, plasma + magnetometer), Kyoto WDC Dst quicklook, planetary Kp, GOES primary magnetometer (independent GEO cross-check).
