Saturn System — The Rings & Their Shepherds

Saturn's rings are not a static disc — they are a billion-particle structure carved, gapped, and waved by the moons orbiting through and beyond them. Why this matters

Resonances build the rings. Saturn's rings look like a smooth disc from afar, but at the meter scale they are a swarm of icy particles on near-circular orbits. The structure — gaps, sharp edges, wave trains — is almost entirely the work of the moons. Wherever a ring particle's orbital period is a simple integer ratio of a moon's, the periodic gravitational tug adds up over many orbits instead of averaging away. The result is a spiral density wave (a tightly-wound traffic jam of particle orbits) or, for the strongest resonances, a cleared gap with a sculpted edge.

Mimas and the Cassini Division. The Cassini Division — the famous dark band you can see in a backyard telescope — is not empty space; it is a region thinned out at the Mimas 2:1 inner Lindblad resonance. A particle there laps Mimas every two of its own orbits, so Mimas pumps its eccentricity until collisions and crowding redistribute it inward. The same resonance holds the B-ring's outer edge in a two-lobed shape that rotates with Mimas — exactly what this page renders.

Embedded moonlets and shepherds. Pan (in the Encke Gap) and Daphnis (in the Keeler Gap) are big enough to bulldoze their own lanes, raising scalloped wakes on the gap edges as they pass. Daphnis's slight inclination lifts vertical waves that threw kilometre-long shadows when the Sun crossed the ring plane at Saturn's 2009 equinox. Prometheus and Pandora bracket the narrow F ring, and Prometheus periodically reaches in to draw out dark "streamer-channels." Janus's 7:6 resonance defines the A-ring's crisp outer edge.

What you're seeing. The ring is a single shader that evaluates the real radial profile plus a set of moon-driven features. Every spiral density-wave train's pattern speed is set to the perturbing moon's mean motion (computed from its semi-major axis), so when you advance the clock the waves co-rotate with the moons that launch them. The moons themselves move on analytic Kepler orbits with first-order J₂ secular precession — Saturn's strong oblateness makes the inner moons' nodes and pericentres turn over in years, so their orbit rings visibly precess. Ring radii are physical (R_S = 60,268 km); wave amplitudes and the gap widths are exaggerated so they read on screen. The ring-opening-to-Sun readout is an approximate seasonal model (29.46-yr period; ring-plane crossings near 2009 and 2025).
