Propulsion & Thrust — Live Telemetry standby

Each engine pumps a near-fixed mass flow, so thrust climbs from its sea-level rating to its higher vacuum rating as the air thins (the nozzle's pₑ−pₐ term grows). The vehicle also sheds a spent stage's structure at burnout, after a brief coast. The integrator models all of it — so thrust, specific impulse, thrust-to-weight and acceleration all evolve live as the rocket climbs and stages.

Aerodynamic Forces — Live Telemetry standby

Drag is split into the three forces a real rocket fights on the way up: skin friction (viscous shear in the turbulent boundary layer), pressure drag (base wake + the nose's subsonic form drag), and wave drag (energy lost to the bow shock once the flow goes transonic). Numbers are engineering correlations — Schlichting skin-friction, Hoerner form factors — fed through the same ascent integrator as the flight. Press Launch to watch them move; the readout below updates live with Mach.
