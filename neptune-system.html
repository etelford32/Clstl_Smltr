For a prograde moon (like our Moon) tides transfer angular momentum outward and the orbit expands. Triton orbits backwards, so the tidal bulge it raises on Neptune lags ahead of it and drains its orbital energy — it spirals in. Integrating da/dt = −(3 k₂/Q)(m_T/M_N)(R_N/a)⁵ n a with Love number k₂ ≈ 0.41 gives a few-billion-year clock; Neptune's tidal Q is poorly known, so drag the slider to see how sensitive the doomsday is. When a reaches the Roche limit, Triton is torn apart into a ring system.

Why Triton matters

A captured world. Triton is the only large moon in the Solar System on a retrograde orbit. It cannot have formed in place from Neptune's disc — it was captured, almost certainly a Kuiper-belt object like Pluto (the two are near-twins in size and density). The capture would have scattered Neptune's original regular satellites, which may explain Nereid's wild eccentricity and the cratering record.

Active and frigid. Voyager 2 (1989) found Triton's surface at −235 °C — the coldest directly measured in the Solar System — yet streaked by active nitrogen geysers and resurfaced by cryovolcanism. A thin nitrogen atmosphere and a "cantaloupe terrain" point to an interior still evolving, plausibly over a subsurface ocean.

What you're seeing. A Newtonian Yoshida-4 integration in Neptune-centric SI units with the central-body J₂ term switched on. The retrograde inclination, the inner-moon precession, and the conjunction pattern all fall out of gravity alone — no scripted motion. Energy (including the J₂ potential) is bounded by the symplectic integrator, so the run stays physical indefinitely.

The rings, the arcs, and the magnetic field

Five rings, named for a discovery. Galle, Le Verrier, Lassell, Arago and Adams honour the people who predicted and found Neptune. They are dark and dusty — invisible from Earth until Voyager — and far fainter than Saturn's.

Arcs that shouldn't exist. The Adams ring isn't smooth: its dust bunches into bright arcs (Fraternité, Égalité, Liberté, Courage) that, left alone, should spread into a uniform ring within years. They survive because the moon Galatea, just interior, holds them in a 42:43 corotation-inclination resonance — a natural confinement mechanism we still study for exoplanet discs.

A tilted, off-centre magnet. Neptune's magnetic dipole is inclined ~47° to its spin axis and offset ~0.55 R_N from the planet's centre — so the field strength at the cloud tops varies by an order of magnitude with longitude. Toggle Dipole: ON to see the field geometry.
