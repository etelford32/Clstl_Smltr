Great Red Spot · shallow-water lab

A 1.5-layer (reduced-gravity) shallow-water model written in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly, advancing live in your browser. The active weather layer is seeded with Jupiter's measured zonal-jet profile; drop in an anticyclone at 22°S and it self-organises into a long-lived oval — the Great Red Spot — while a cyclone of equal strength is strained apart by the very same shear.

What you're seeing. Warm = anticyclonic, cool = cyclonic vorticity. The bands are the measured jets (from the same profile that drives jupiter-system); the oval is the seeded vortex. The seed sits in anticyclonic shear at 22°S, which is exactly why an anticyclone survives there and a cyclone does not (Marcus 1988; Dowling & Ingersoll 1989). Numerics: Arakawa C-grid · Sadourny energy-conserving advection · SSP-RK2 · biharmonic hyperviscosity · periodic in longitude, sponged walls in latitude. The model is a real PDE solver, not a texture; it is intentionally a reduced model (one active layer, beta-plane), not a global GCM. Rebuild with ./scripts/build-wasm.sh.
