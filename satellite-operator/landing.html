The wedge

The Sun doesn't care about your forecast model.

Empirical models work — until they don't. JB2008. NRLMSIS. Regional Kp forecasts. The dominant tools across the industry are calibrated on quiet conditions and break down precisely when operators need them most.

Storms are when the physics matters. Storms are when empirical models fail.

February 2022 NRLMSIS reported nominal density. SpaceX lost 38 Starlinks in a single launch.
May 2024 — the Gannon storm Nearly 5,000 simultaneous maneuvers across the LEO constellation. Operators reacted. They didn't predict.

Parkers Physics runs the actual physics. Magnetohydrodynamics, on your orbit, refreshed continuously. So when the storm hits, you already know what your satellite will feel.

How it works

Three layers. One prediction.

01 — INGEST

Live, always on

Solar wind from ACE and DSCOVR. X-ray flux from GOES-16/18. CMEs from NASA DONKI. Magnetometer data from the global INTERMAGNET network. Twelve feeds, refreshed continuously.

02 — PHYSICS

SWMF, productized

The same magnetohydrodynamics code NASA's Community Coordinated Modeling Center uses for research. We containerized it, gave it a real-time pipeline, and wrapped it in an API your flight dynamics team can actually call.

03 — DECIDE

Per-satellite output

You give us your ephemeris. We give you drag, radiation, and surface-charging predictions specific to your spacecraft — with quantified uncertainty bounds. Not point estimates pretending to be certainty.

Generic models output grids. Parkers Physics outputs decisions.
